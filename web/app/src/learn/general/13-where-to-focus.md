# 13. Where to focus: research and career

**In simple terms.** This chapter brings together the grid problem (Chapters 2–3), the research gaps (Chapters 4–9 and 12) and the market lessons (Chapters 10–11) into a practical recommendation.

## 13.1 Market signals in one view

- **Battery optimisation attracts the most capital**: Entrix (€43 million), Sympower and Suena (€27 million combined), Green Voltis (€13.5 million), Capalo AI (€11 million). Strong for jobs, hard to enter as a newcomer.
- **Dutch congestion-focused energy management is crowded** with young startups (Tibo, Eddy Grid, iwell, Zympler, Currentt). iwell's coverage cites about 12,000 Dutch businesses waiting for a grid connection (a count of businesses, whereas the 15,000 in Section 3.1 counts requests) ([ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/?p=4980)).
- **Depot energy-as-a-service is growing fast** (Decade Energy, Delta Charge), driven by truck and van electrification behind small connections.
- **Basic load management is a commodity**, built into chargers from ABB, Kempower and Siemens.
- **Flexible data centres are the newest, largest-ticket segment**, led by Emerald AI and the AEMA alliance, mostly in the US.
- **Skills are scarce** (Chapter 14).

Funding rounds signal investor belief, not proven profitable markets.

## 13.2 Segment comparison

| Segment | Employability | Competition | Fit with this profile |

## 13.3 Recommended focus: reliable flexibility for EV-heavy sites

The strongest overlap of research gap, market need and personal fit is **reliable flexibility for EV-heavy business sites**: logistics depots, charging hubs, retail and fuel-station sites with batteries. Concretely:

1. **Planning**: battery size and contract choice with a stated probability of breaching the grid limit (gap G4, Section 12.2).
2. **Operation**: an uncertainty-aware controller that keeps the promise, including battery state-of-charge and charging-curve uncertainty (gap G3).
3. **Digital twin**: one site model, calibrated on real data, used for both (gap G1).

Why this combination works:

- **Employability hedge.** The same skills are exactly what battery optimisers, charging-software companies and integrators hire for.
- **An uncrowded gap.** Integrators run thousands of feasibility studies (Section 11.3), and battery optimisers such as Entrix and Furo already bundle sizing with operation. What none of the companies reviewed here prominently offers is a quantified reliability tied to a flexible grid contract; that, not sizing itself, is the open question.

## 13.5 What to avoid

- **Building another generic controller or EMS**: crowded and commoditised (Section 11.4).
- **Pure energy trading as a newcomer**: capital-intensive, dominated by scale.
- **Data centres without a partner**: high upside, hard access; better as a later extension of the same site model.

## 13.6 Decision rules and next steps

**Decision rules.** If the main goal becomes a high salary soon, target battery optimisers and charging-software companies. Otherwise, follow the research route and keep testing the market.

**Next steps.**

1. Run 10–15 interviews: a depot integrator (such as Decade Energy), a charging-software company (such as The Mobility House), a Dutch EMS startup (such as Tibo or Eddy Grid), a charge point operator, an energy consultant and a grid operator. Ask how they size batteries, what goes wrong, and whether a quantified reliability would change contract decisions.
2. Start a three-month project that produces a visible result: probabilistic battery sizing for a charging site on open Dutch data (Section 9.3).

## 13.7 Chinese hardware and European rules

- **Chinese hardware dominates.** Chinese system integrators held 76% of the global battery storage market in 2025, and Sungrow, BYD and Huawei hold the top three positions in Europe ([Wood Mackenzie, 2026](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/2026-bess-market-share-chinese-integrators-76-percent-global-dominance-2025/)). The ten largest storage cell suppliers, all Chinese, shipped 83.3% of cells in the first half of 2026 ([Benchmark](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/who-shipped-the-most-bess-cells-and-systems-in-h1-2026-)).
- **Europe is tightening the rules.** Since April 2026, EU-funded projects may not use solar or battery power conversion systems from "high-risk" countries, with requirements applying to grid-connected projects from November 2026 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/081326-eu-pv-storage-inverter-funding-ban-could-reflect-shift-toward-clean-energy)). Lithuania banned remote access by Chinese companies to solar, wind and storage installations above 100 kW in November 2024 ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/08/eu-expert-group-urges-curbs-on-high-risk-solar-suppliers/)). Renewable auctions must apply non-price criteria, including cybersecurity and resilience, to at least 30% of auctioned capacity from 30 December 2025 ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/?p=16001)).
- **Compliance is getting heavier.** From 18 February 2027, every industrial battery above 2 kWh placed on the EU market needs a digital battery passport, which also covers stationary storage ([Z2Data](https://www.z2data.com/insights/why-the-eu-digital-product-passport-will-reshape-compliance-for-battery-manufacturers-in-2027)).

The open need sits in the gap between cheap, capable Chinese hardware and European buyers who need it to be secure, compliant, reliable and bankable.
