# 1. Scope and how to read this guide

**In simple terms.** In many places the electricity grid is full, so businesses that want more power have to wait for years. Batteries, smart EV charging and other flexible equipment can help them connect sooner, if they can reliably limit their power when the grid is busy. This guide explains the problem from the ground up, reviews what research and industry already do about it, and ends with a practical view on where to focus and which skills pay off.

This guide is a working map of one fast-moving space: **electricity grid congestion, and the flexibility of business sites that can help solve it**. A "site" is everything behind one grid connection: a battery, solar panels and controllable consumption such as EV chargers, heat pumps or data-centre computing. The guide has four goals:

1. **Understand the grid** and why it is congested.
2. **Know the state of the art** in research: how flexibility is measured, modelled, controlled and planned, and where the gaps are.
3. **Know the market**: which companies and startups are active, what they do, and what can be learned from them.
4. **Choose a direction**: what to focus on for research or a well-paid career, and which skills to build.

Chapter 15 lists key references, Appendix A is an in-depth primer on batteries and EV batteries, Appendix B covers battery energy storage systems with a focus on DC-coupled storage for EV charging, and a glossary of terms closes the guide. Every chapter opens with an "In simple terms" summary, then the detail, then an assessment. Figures and worked examples explain the main ideas and methods; numbers in worked examples are labelled illustrative unless a source is given. Short on time? Read Chapters 2 and 3, the "In simple terms" summaries, Section 11.4 (lessons from the market) and Chapters 13 and 14.

One question runs through the whole guide: **how much flexibility can a site reliably promise under a flexible grid contract, and how should it be planned and operated to keep that promise?** It is where the grid problem, the research gaps and the market opportunity meet.

Where the guide says "this research", it means a research programme built around that question, set out in Section 12.3 and Chapter 13.

Sources are peer-reviewed papers, preprints, regulatory documents, company announcements and industry press up to November 2026, with a focus on Europe and the Netherlands. Funding and traction figures come from company announcements and press, and are signals rather than audited facts. Where a claim rests on a vendor or press source, this is stated.
