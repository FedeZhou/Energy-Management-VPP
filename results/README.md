## Baseline scoreboard (2026-11-07, code commit `11463fd`)

[baseline_scoreboard.md](baseline_scoreboard.md): CityLearn 2022 phase 1, 5 buildings, one year.

| Controller | Cost vs no battery | Site peak (kW) | Battery cycles / year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No battery (PV only) | 1.000 (8,779) | 16.7 | 0 |
| Idle battery (sanity check) | 1.000 | 16.7 | 0 |
| CityLearn BasicRBC | **1.110** (worse) | 17.0 | 229 |
| CityLearn BasicBatteryRBC | **0.906** | 15.0 | 226 |
| CityLearn OptimizedRBC | **1.026** (worse) | 15.9 | 91 |

What this tells us:

1. **The idle controller reproduces the no-battery baseline exactly**, so our KPI pipeline and
   our reading of CityLearn's baseline are consistent.
2. **Only the solar-aware rule saves money (~9.4%).** BasicBatteryRBC charges 06:00-14:00 (when
   PV is high) and discharges the rest of the day.
3. **Two of CityLearn's stock rules lose money.** BasicRBC and OptimizedRBC were designed for
   heat-pump thermal storage: they charge from the grid at night and discharge during the day,
   when PV already covers the load, so the stored energy is exported for nothing and losses
   are paid. This is the "rules are brittle when the site or tariff changes" point from
   docs/research.md, seen live.
4. **None of the rules targets the 16:00-21:00 price peak directly.** That is the obvious room
   an MPC controller (task 4) should exploit; the gap between 0.906 and whatever MPC reaches
   is the first number to watch.
5. ~226 equivalent full cycles per year (about 0.6 per day) for the best rule. Whether that
   wear is worth 825 per year of savings is what the wear-cost model (tasks 2-3) will answer.

Caveats: price currency assumed USD (not stated in the dataset); exports earn nothing
(CityLearn's assumption); 8,759 of 8,760 hours are scored (CityLearn does not record the last
step).
